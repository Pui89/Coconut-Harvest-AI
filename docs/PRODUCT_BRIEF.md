# Product brief — Coconut Harvest AI

**Product direction:** A local-first AI application paired with an agricultural-robot system, developed toward a dependable coconut perception and harvest-assistance product. Today it remains a prototype and simulation-first engineering project.

## The problem
Coconut harvesting involves difficult visibility, height, access and handling conditions. A useful robotic system must do more than detect a coconut: it must localize the target, establish whether a planned action is feasible, handle uncertainty and stop safely when evidence is insufficient.

## What the project provides today
- A software prototype separating vision and robot-workflow concerns.
- Local-first workflow and audit-oriented outputs.
- Candidate-image collection, annotation/conversion and dataset-audit tools.
- Simulation-oriented decision workflow and automated regression tests.

## What is not yet a product claim
The project has not demonstrated a trained detector's independent accuracy, calibrated real-camera localization, safe physical cutting, orchard field performance, customer savings or commercial superiority. Current artwork is concept illustration. Do not sell it as a ready-to-deploy autonomous harvesting robot.

## Potential first customers and collaborators
Agricultural research institutes, university robotics groups, coconut grower cooperatives, agricultural equipment integrators and public innovation programmes. These are audiences to validate, not confirmed customers.

## Why evaluate it
The project is intended to make a complex harvesting-robot problem inspectable: data provenance, reproducible tests, localization, task feasibility and safety gates are explicit rather than hidden behind a polished demo.

## Demonstration plan
1. Show the application/workflow and distinguish recorded data, synthetic examples and concept artwork.
2. Show the dataset review and annotation process.
3. Run the baseline only after the dataset is approved; show the exact held-out metrics and failure cases.
4. Show simulation checks and clearly identify unvalidated physical capabilities.
5. Invite a partner to define orchard-specific requirements and a supervised feasibility study.

## Evidence required before a paid pilot
- Reproducible installation and test results.
- Licensed, reviewed dataset and frozen test split.
- Independent perception/localization measurements.
- Simulation safety report and risk assessment.
- Partner-defined acceptance criteria, support plan and total-cost model.

## 30 / 60 / 90-day targets
- **30 days:** preserve the candidate artifact, finish source review and annotation, audit dataset splits.
- **60 days:** run a reproducible baseline and publish metrics, error cases and latency.
- **90 days:** complete localization/simulation validation plan and conduct structured customer discovery. Dates are targets, not promises of completion.

## Product positioning
Coconut Harvest AI is intended to help agricultural partners evaluate a complete workflow—from finding visible coconuts to estimating target location, checking task feasibility, and stopping when evidence is insufficient. The product direction is practical and measurable: local installation, transparent logs, repeatable benchmarks, clear operator controls, and a staged path from software to simulation and supervised hardware trials. This is not yet a ready-to-deploy harvesting robot; customer value, performance, safety and savings must be measured with qualified partners before commercial claims are made.
